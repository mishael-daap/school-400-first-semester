The labs here work differently from labs you may have had before. There is no one standing over you in the room while you build. You build at home, then your classmates test you. The idea comes from the 42 school, and it works because explaining your own model out loud is how you find out whether you really understand it.

The rhythm of a lab
Read the brief. Every lab has a mandatory part, a bonus part and a correction sheet of yes-or-no checks. The correction sheet is printed in the brief, so you know exactly what you will be asked.

Build between Tuesdays, wherever you like. The two laboratory hours are for evaluation, not for building.

Run the tests. Each lab is a programming exercise on Whollify. Press Run to try the visible tests as often as you want. Some tests stay hidden and only run when you submit.

Submit before the lock. The lock is 18:00 on the Monday before the evaluation. Late work scores zero for that lab.

Get evaluated on Tuesday. Two rounds of about fifty minutes, by classmates from your own group whom Whollify picks at random. You drive the laptop and they ask the questions. Expect to be asked to change a parameter on the spot and say what the new result means.

Record a short answer video. Within forty-eight hours a classmate from the other group evaluates a short recording in which you answer the brief's fixed questions about your model.

What the tests do and do not mean
Passing the tests means your code does what the brief asked. It is not your lab mark. Your mark comes from the three evaluations, and an item you cannot explain is marked no, even if the code runs perfectly.

How your mark is worked out
Your lab mark is the reliability-weighted mean of the three evaluations, as Whollify computes it. If two evaluators differ by more than two marks, a demonstrator re-marks. Demonstrators also re-mark a random ten percent of every round, and the facilitator settles disputes.

Labs 0 to 5 work as a gate. Pass at least four of the six with fifty percent or more, or you will not receive your Solo Build and Group Capstone marks.

Being a good evaluator
How you evaluate is itself assessed. Marks the audit finds unjustified cost you participation marks and reduce your weight afterwards. Colluding on marks, evaluating a file other than the locked one, or not turning up for an assigned evaluation is malpractice.

What every submission must carry
Put this at the top of your code as comments. The starter code already has the space for it.

Your Class Number (most important), the date, your matriculation number, your group (CS-A or CS-B) and your programme.

No name. Leaving this block out or adding your name costs 2 marks.

A fixed random seed, so your evaluator can reproduce your numbers.

One line declaring any generative AI you used and what you used it for. You may use these tools. The live defence is the check: if you cannot explain and change your own program when asked, you get no credit for it, whoever or whatever wrote it.

Tools
You code in Python 3.8 with the standard library on Whollify, so your submitted code should not need anything you have to install. For your own plots and analysis you can use whatever you like on your laptop.

Arena runs on Windows and needs a licence. If you do not have a Windows machine, use the University computer laboratories outside class hours, or the cloud Windows machines provided through the Oracle Academy membership. Sort this out before Week 4.

In the laboratory, laptops are required and phones stay silent and out of sight. If it is not on Whollify, it is not official.
