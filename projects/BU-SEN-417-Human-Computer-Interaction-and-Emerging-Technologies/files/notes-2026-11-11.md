## User-Centered Design (UCD)

UCD is an approach to designing interactive systems in which the needs, expectations, abilities, limitations and experiences of users are considered throughout the development and design process.

Key requirements:

- Focus on people and their tasks.
- Design from evidence about users.
- Involve users throughout.
- Test, learn and improve repeatedly.

## Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics

1. **Visibility of system status** — keep users informed about what is going on through timely feedback.
2. **Match between the system and the real world** — speak the users' language and follow real-world conventions.
3. **User control and freedom** — offer undo and redo, and a way out of unwanted states.
4. **Consistency and standards** — the same words and actions mean the same thing; follow platform conventions.
5. **Error prevention** — design out error-prone conditions before relying on error messages.
6. **Recognition rather than recall** — make objects, actions and options visible so users do not have to remember them.
7. **Flexibility and efficiency of use** — accelerators for experts, defaults for novices.
8. **Aesthetic and minimalist design** — no irrelevant or rarely needed information competing for attention.
9. **Help users recognize, diagnose and recover from errors** — plain-language messages that state the problem and suggest a fix.
10. **Help and documentation** — easy to search, focused on the task, and written as concrete steps.

## Categories of Users

- **Primary users** use the system directly.
- **Secondary users** use it occasionally.
- **Tertiary users** may not use it directly but are affected by its operations or output.
